## Chapter 14: The Autobahn of Ghosts

The holographic map shimmered above the pit lane, a ghost of neon green and electric blue. Berlin. The starting grid was a concrete scar left by the old Tempelhof Airport, runways stretching into the dark like frozen rivers. Ten crews, nine cars. The Hokkaido Drifters had been sent home in Amsterdam with their Nissan Skyline stripped to a rolling chassis, and the absence of their orange-and-black livery left a hole in the grid that everyone pretended not to notice.

Cable’s voice crackled through the comms, tinny and sharp. "Berlin. Leg four. They're calling it the Autobahn of Ghosts. GPS goes dark in the eastern sector. No checkpoint markers until you're inside the kill zone."

The Blaze Twins sat in their modified Audi RS6, the engine idling with a low, predatory rumble. Dex Blaze gripped the wheel, knuckles white, his twin sister Sable riding shotgun with a tablet wired into a satellite uplink that was already flickering with static. Their car was a scarred veteran of three continents — carbon fiber patches over bullet holes from Mexico City, a cracked headlight taped with electrical tape from a Hong Kong rail-yard scrape.

"Ghosts," Dex muttered. "I don't like anything that can't be outrun."

"Nothing outruns us," Sable said, not looking up from the screen. "Not even the dead."

The countdown hologram flickered to life above the starting line — a massive, translucent skull with a timer burning in its eye sockets. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. The other crews revved engines like caged animals. El Tapado's matte-black Porsche 911 sat in pole position, its driver's visor reflecting the skull's glow, revealing nothing. Vega, the ex-cop, was in a silver Corvette two rows back, his partner a quiet man named Reyes who never spoke unless it was about vectors or tire pressure. The Mechanic's Frankenstein machine — a Toyota Supra welded together from three different wrecks, held together by prayer and JB Weld — coughed smoke from its exhaust like a dying dragon.

Three. Two. One.

The skull’s jaw unhinged in a silent scream, and the world became velocity.

Engines roared, tires screamed, and the pack surged down the runway like a stampede of metal. The Blaze Twins cut to the inside immediately, Sable calling out lines from memory. "Follow the second runway, merge onto the B96, then hard left at the radio tower. The old Nordschleife section is closed but the perimeter road is open — we can shave two minutes if we're brave."

Dex grinned. "Brave is my middle name."

"Your middle name is 'Dangerous Idiot,'" Sable said. "But we'll call it brave."

The city swallowed them — graffiti-covered tenement blocks, the skeletal dome of a bombed church, a river of sodium lights bleeding into the wet asphalt. The eastern sector was a dead zone, a ghost of the Wall still running through the pavement like a scar. GPS died at the invisible border. Sable's tablet went black, the satellite uplink severed.

"Navigation's gone," she said, her voice flat. "We're flying blind."

"Good," Dex said. "The blind drive faster."

Behind them, the pack had split. El Tapado vanished into a side street with a flick of his rear wing. The Mechanic's Supra was holding third, smoke trailing like a banner. Vega's Corvette was glued to their bumper, the ex-cop's headlights filling the Audi's mirrors like malevolent eyes.

Ahead, the road split into three tunnels — concrete mouths yawning in the dark. No signs. No holograms. No checkpoints. Just three choices, each one a gamble.

"Pick one," Dex said.

Sable stared at the tunnels, her mind a compass spinning without a pole. "The middle one. It smells like diesel and fear."

"Great reason."

They dove into the center tunnel. The concrete swallowed the sound of their engine, amplified it, bounced it back until the world was a roar. Water dripped from the ceiling in fat, cold drops. The walls were covered in graffiti — tags, names, dates, some of them old enough to be relics. And then, in the middle of the tunnel, a hologram flickered to life.

A skull. Smaller than the one at the start, but unmistakable. Its eyes glowed amber, and beneath it, a text rendered in brutal sans-serif:

**GHOST CHECKPOINT 1 — 3.2 KM — GAP: 1.7M**

A jump. In a tunnel.

"Are you kidding me?" Dex shouted.

The tunnel ahead ended in a sheer drop — a construction pit, maybe thirty feet across, with a ramp of rusted steel jutting from the lip at a suicidal angle. Beyond the gap, the tunnel resumed, the exit glowing with pale city light. There was no way around. No alternative route. The race had built a wall of air and gravity, and the only way through was to fly.

"Hit it full," Sable said, her voice steady. "Engine brake at the last second, then punch it off the lip. We need 1.7 meters. We can clear it."

"That's a big 'can.'"

"Life's a gamble, Dex. Drive."

He floored it. The Audi surged, the engine screaming in sixth gear, the tunnel walls blurring into a smear of concrete and shadow. The gap loomed, a black chasm swallowing the road. Dex downshifted, the engine backfiring like gunfire, then slammed the throttle at the lip. The Audi launched, tires spinning in empty air, the nose tilting up, up, up, and for a heart-stopping moment, they were a missile aimed at the opposite wall.

The landing was brutal — a crash of suspension, a screech of metal, sparks showering the tunnel as the Audi bottomed out and bounced. Dex wrestled the wheel, fought the car back into a straight line, and they were through.

"Fifty centimeters to spare," Sable said, exhaling. "Nice."

"Fifty centimeters is a mile in my book."

The tunnel spat them out into a maze of industrial lots — cranes silhouetted against the dawn, shipping containers stacked like metal tombs, the smell of oil and wet gravel. The Autobahn of Ghosts was a graveyard of infrastructure, a place where the city had died and been left to rot. And somewhere in the maze, other crews were doing the same thing — surviving, gambling, pushing metal past its limits.

The comms crackled. A transmission from the race director, filtered through a voice modulator that made it sound like gravel in a blender. "Ghost checkpoint two. The Wall. Three meters tall. Two lanes of traffic. The slowest crew to reach the wall will be stripped of their vehicle and sent home. You have eight minutes. Go."

"The Wall," Sable repeated. "They're going to make us jump the Berlin Wall."

"Or through it," Dex said, eyeing a forklift parked near a rusted container. "That thing looks heavy enough to punch through."

"Too slow. We jump."

They found the Wall — a section of the original, preserved and painted with murals of doves and barbed wire, stretching across the industrial lot like a gravestone. Two lanes of traffic had been set up on either side, coned off, with ramps already in place. The other crews were there, engines idling, waiting. The Mechanic's Supra was missing a front bumper. Vega's Corvette had a cracked windshield. El Tapado's Porsche was untouched, immaculate, as if it had been driven by a ghost.

"Eight minutes," the director repeated. "Go."

The crews surged. The Blaze Twins took the inside lane, Dex hitting the ramp at 180 kilometers per hour. The Audi flew, clearing the Wall with a meter to spare, landing in a shower of sparks and dust. Vega was right behind them, the Corvette hitting the ramp with a violent lurch, its nose clipping the top of the Wall — a shower of red paint and fiberglass — but it cleared. The Mechanic didn't. The Supra hit the ramp too slow, the rear end clipping the Wall, and the car cartwheeled, landing on its roof in a screaming shower of glass and metal.

The comms went silent. The Supra didn't move.

"Mechanic's down," Sable said, her voice flat. "That's two crews eliminated."

"Three," Dex said, pointing. El Tapado's Porsche was gone — vanished into a side alley without touching the Wall. Clever. The masked driver had found a gap, a hole in the Wall's preservation, just wide enough for a car. He'd slipped through like a shadow.

The race wasn't about being fastest. It was about being smartest. And El Tapado was playing chess while everyone else was playing bumper cars.

The final checkpoint materialized ahead — a massive holographic skull hovering over a rusted bridge that spanned the Spree River. The finish line. But between them and the bridge, a gauntlet of obstacles: burning barrels, spike strips, a line of abandoned buses. The Autobahn of Ghosts was designed to kill, and the last hundred meters were the most lethal.

"Full send," Dex said.

"Full send," Sable agreed.

The Audi tore through the gauntlet — a barrel exploded on their left, showering the windshield with fire; spikes scraped the undercarriage, sending a violent shudder through the frame; a bus loomed ahead, and Dex swerved, clipping its rear bumper hard enough to spin the car 180 degrees. They faced the wrong way, the bridge behind them, the finish line waiting. For a moment, the world was silence.

Then Dex slammed the throttle, yanked the wheel, and the Audi spun back around, facing the bridge. The finish line was 200 meters away. Vega's Corvette was closing fast on their left. El Tapado's Porsche was a black blur on the right.

One hundred meters. Fifty. Twenty.

The Audi crossed the line three car lengths ahead of the Corvette. El Tapado's Porsche came in second, its driver's visor turning briefly toward the Audi — a nod, perhaps, or a warning. Vega finished third, his silver car smoking, his face unreadable through the cracked windshield.

The holographic skull above the bridge dissolved into a shower of golden light, and a new message appeared:

**LEG FOUR COMPLETE. THREE CREWS ELIMINATED. SEVEN REMAIN.**
**NEXT LEG: TOKYO. THE NIGHT RACE.**
**BRING LIGHT. BRING SPEED. BRING YOUR DEAD.**

Dex killed the engine. The Audi ticked and cooled in the Berlin dawn, its paint scarred, its frame bent, its heart still beating. Sable set down her tablet and looked at her brother, a rare smile cracking her face.

"Tokyo," she said. "We get to do this again."

"Best job in the world," Dex said.

Behind them, the Autobahn of Ghosts was already fading into memory, the concrete and the Wall and the fire all collapsing into a single, beautiful, impossible moment of speed. Seven crews remained. Six more legs. One golden garage.

And the Blaze Twins were still in the race.
