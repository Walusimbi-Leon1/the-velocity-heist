The Velocity Heist

Floor It. Or Be Forgotten.

Ten crews. One continent. A golden garage worth more than a country. Each leg is a timed, no-rules race through a different city — drift through Hong Kong, jump the canals of Amsterdam, outrun the law in Mexico City. The slowest crew every round is stripped of their car and sent home. The first crew to the final garage keeps the keys, the cars, and the crown. Speed is the only mercy.

Chapter 14: The Autobahn of Ghosts

The holographic map shimmered above the pit lane, a ghost of neon green and electric blue. Berlin. The starting grid was a concrete scar left by the old Tempelhof Airport, runways stretching into the dark like frozen rivers. Ten crews, nine cars. The Hokkaido Drifters had been sent home in Amsterdam with their Nissan Skyline stripped to a rolling chassis, and the absence of their orange-and-black livery left a hole in the grid that everyone pretended not to notice.

Cable’s voice crackled through the comms, tinny and sharp. "Berlin. Leg four. They're calling it the Autobahn of Ghosts. GPS goes dark in the eastern sector. No checkpoint markers until you're inside the kill zone."

The Blaze Twins sat in their modified Audi RS6, the engine idling with a low, predatory rumble. Dex Blaze gripped the wheel, knuckles white, his twin sister Sable riding shotgun with a tablet wired into a satellite uplink that was already flickering with static. Their car was a scarred veteran of three continents — carbon fiber patches over bullet holes from Mexico City, a cracked headlight taped with electrical tape from a Hong Kong rail-yard scrape.

"Ghosts," Dex muttered. "I don't like anything that can't be outrun."

"Nothing outruns us," Sable said, not looking up from the screen. "Not even the dead."

The countdown hologram flickered to life above the starting line — a massive, translucent skull with a timer burning in its eye sockets. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. The other crews revved engines like caged animals. El Tapado's matte-black Porsche 911 sat in pole position, its driver's visor reflecting the skull's glow, revealing nothing. Vega, the ex-cop, was in a silver Corvette two rows back, his partner a quiet man named Reyes who never spoke unless it was about vectors or tire pressure. The Mechanic's Frankenstein machine — a Toyota Supra welded together from three different wrecks, held together by prayer and JB Weld — coughed smoke from its exhaust like a dying dragon.

Three. Two. One.

The skull’s jaw unhinged in a silent scream, and the world became velocity.

Engines roared, tires screamed, and the pack surged down the runway like a stampede of metal. The Blaze Twins cut to the inside immediately, Sable calling out lines from memory. "Follow the second runway, merge onto the B96, then hard left at the radio tower. The old Nordschleife section is closed but the perimeter road is open — we can shave two minutes if we're brave."

Dex grinned. "Brave is my middle name."

"Your middle name is 'Dangerous Idiot,'" Sable said. "But we'll call it brave."

The city swallowed them — graffiti-covered tenement blocks, the skeletal dome of a bombed church, a river of sodium lights bleeding into the wet asphalt. The eastern sector was a dead zone, a ghost of the Wall still running through the pavement like a scar. GPS died at the invisible border. Sable's tablet went black, the satellite uplink severed.

"Navigation's gone," she said, her voice flat. "We're flying blind."

"Good," Dex said. "The blind drive faster."

Behind them, the pack had split. El Tapado vanished into a side street with a flick of his rear wing. The Mechanic's Supra was holding third, smoke trailing like a banner. Vega's Corvette was glued to their bumper, the ex-cop's headlights filling the Audi's mirrors like malevolent eyes.

Ahead, the road split into three tunnels — concrete mouths yawning in the dark. No signs. No holograms. No checkpoints. Just three choices, each one a gamble.

"Pick one," Dex said.

Sable stared at the tunnels, her mind a compass spinning without a pole. "The middle one. It smells like diesel and fear."

"Great reason."

They dove into the center tunnel. The concrete swallowed the sound of their engine, amplified it, bounced it back until the world was a roar. Water dripped from the ceiling in fat, cold drops. The walls were covered in graffiti — tags, names, dates, some of them old enough to be relics. And then, in the middle of the tunnel, a hologram flickered to life.

A skull. Smaller than the one at the start, but unmistakable. Its eyes glowed amber, and beneath it, a text rendered in brutal sans-serif:

GHOST CHECKPOINT 1 — 3.2 KM — GAP: 1.7M

A jump. In a tunnel.

"Are you kidding me?" Dex shouted.

The tunnel ahead ended in a sheer drop — a construction pit, maybe thirty feet across, with a ramp of rusted steel jutting from the lip at a suicidal angle. Beyond the gap, the tunnel resumed, the exit glowing with pale city light. There was no way around. No alternative route. The race had built a wall of air and gravity, and the only way through was to fly.

"Hit it full," Sable said, her voice steady. "Engine brake at the last second, then punch it off the lip. We need 1.7 meters. We can clear it."

"That's a big 'can.'"

"Life's a gamble, Dex. Drive."

He floored it. The Audi surged, the engine screaming in sixth gear, the tunnel walls blurring into a smear of concrete and shadow. The gap loomed, a black chasm swallowing the road. Dex downshifted, the engine backfiring like gunfire, then slammed the throttle at the lip. The Audi launched, tires spinning in empty air, the nose tilting up, up, up, and for a heart-stopping moment, they were a missile aimed at the opposite wall.

The landing was brutal — a crash of suspension, a screech of metal, sparks showering the tunnel as the Audi bottomed out and bounced. Dex wrestled the wheel, fought the car back into a straight line, and they were through.

"Fifty centimeters to spare," Sable said, exhaling. "Nice."

"Fifty centimeters is a mile in my book."

The tunnel spat them out into a maze of industrial lots — cranes silhouetted against the dawn, shipping containers stacked like metal tombs, the smell of oil and wet gravel. The Autobahn of Ghosts was a graveyard of infrastructure, a place where the city had died and been left to rot. And somewhere in the maze, other crews were doing the same thing — surviving, gambling, pushing metal past its limits.

The comms crackled. A transmission from the race director, filtered through a voice modulator that made it sound like gravel in a blender. "Ghost checkpoint two. The Wall. Three meters tall. Two lanes of traffic. The slowest crew to reach the wall will be stripped of their vehicle and sent home. You have eight minutes. Go."

"The Wall," Sable repeated. "They're going to make us jump the Berlin Wall."

"Or through it," Dex said, eyeing a forklift parked near a rusted container. "That thing looks heavy enough to punch through."

"Too slow. We jump."

They found the Wall — a section of the original, preserved and painted with murals of doves and barbed wire, stretching across the industrial lot like a gravestone. Two lanes of traffic had been set up on either side, coned off, with ramps already in place. The other crews were there, engines idling, waiting. The Mechanic's Supra was missing a front bumper. Vega's Corvette had a cracked windshield. El Tapado's Porsche was untouched, immaculate, as if it had been driven by a ghost.

"Eight minutes," the director repeated. "Go."

The crews surged. The Blaze Twins took the inside lane, Dex hitting the ramp at 180 kilometers per hour. The Audi flew, clearing the Wall with a meter to spare, landing in a shower of sparks and dust. Vega was right behind them, the Corvette hitting the ramp with a violent lurch, its nose clipping the top of the Wall — a shower of red paint and fiberglass — but it cleared. The Mechanic didn't. The Supra hit the ramp too slow, the rear end clipping the Wall, and the car cartwheeled, landing on its roof in a screaming shower of glass and metal.

The comms went silent. The Supra didn't move.

"Mechanic's down," Sable said, her voice flat. "That's two crews eliminated."

"Three," Dex said, pointing. El Tapado's Porsche was gone — vanished into a side alley without touching the Wall. Clever. The masked driver had found a gap, a hole in the Wall's preservation, just wide enough for a car. He'd slipped through like a shadow.

The race wasn't about being fastest. It was about being smartest. And El Tapado was playing chess while everyone else was playing bumper cars.

The final checkpoint materialized ahead — a massive holographic skull hovering over a rusted bridge that spanned the Spree River. The finish line. But between them and the bridge, a gauntlet of obstacles: burning barrels, spike strips, a line of abandoned buses. The Autobahn of Ghosts was designed to kill, and the last hundred meters were the most lethal.

"Full send," Dex said.

"Full send," Sable agreed.

The Audi tore through the gauntlet — a barrel exploded on their left, showering the windshield with fire; spikes scraped the undercarriage, sending a violent shudder through the frame; a bus loomed ahead, and Dex swerved, clipping its rear bumper hard enough to spin the car 180 degrees. They faced the wrong way, the bridge behind them, the finish line waiting. For a moment, the world was silence.

Then Dex slammed the throttle, yanked the wheel, and the Audi spun back around, facing the bridge. The finish line was 200 meters away. Vega's Corvette was closing fast on their left. El Tapado's Porsche was a black blur on the right.

One hundred meters. Fifty. Twenty.

The Audi crossed the line three car lengths ahead of the Corvette. El Tapado's Porsche came in second, its driver's visor turning briefly toward the Audi — a nod, perhaps, or a warning. Vega finished third, his silver car smoking, his face unreadable through the cracked windshield.

The holographic skull above the bridge dissolved into a shower of golden light, and a new message appeared:

LEG FOUR COMPLETE. THREE CREWS ELIMINATED. SEVEN REMAIN.

NEXT LEG: TOKYO. THE NIGHT RACE.

BRING LIGHT. BRING SPEED. BRING YOUR DEAD.

Dex killed the engine. The Audi ticked and cooled in the Berlin dawn, its paint scarred, its frame bent, its heart still beating. Sable set down her tablet and looked at her brother, a rare smile cracking her face.

"Tokyo," she said. "We get to do this again."

"Best job in the world," Dex said.

Behind them, the Autobahn of Ghosts was already fading into memory, the concrete and the Wall and the fire all collapsing into a single, beautiful, impossible moment of speed. Seven crews remained. Six more legs. One golden garage.

And the Blaze Twins were still in the race.

Chapter 15 — Tokyo: The Neon Gauntlet

The cargo plane swallowed them whole at dawn, a rusted Antonov that smelled of jet fuel and old sweat. The Audi sat strapped to the deck, its tires chocked, its scars gleaming under the industrial lights. Dex ran a hand along the driver's door, feeling the dents from Berlin, the scratches from Hong Kong, the memory of every near-death painted into the metal like topography.

"Stop petting it," Sable said, not looking up from her tablet. "You'll make it nervous."

"It's a machine. It doesn't get nervous."

"Neither do you, and yet you've been chewing the same piece of gum for three hours."

Dex spat the gum into his palm, looked at it, and flicked it across the cargo hold. "Fair."

The other crews were scattered across the plane, each with their own corner, their own rituals. Vega sat alone near the tail, running a whetstone along a knife he'd produced from somewhere—the ex-cop's eyes fixed on the horizon through a porthole, seeing something none of them could. The Mechanic's Supra was gone, stripped and sold for scrap in Berlin, but the Mechanic himself was still here—a wiry man with welding scars on his forearms, sitting cross-legged beside a crate of tools he no longer needed. He'd refused the offer to join another crew. "My car died," he'd said. "I'm just here to watch."

El Tapado was in the cockpit, or so the flight crew claimed. No one had seen him board. His Porsche sat in the center of the hold, pristine, untouchable, as if it had driven itself up the ramp.

"Seven crews," Sable said, scrolling through her tablet. "The others are unknowns. Mercenaries. One crew from Dubai—twin brothers like us, but they drive a Bugatti. Rich kids. Another from Brazil in a modified Evo. And a pair from Russia in a Lada that should not be able to do one hundred and fifty miles an hour, and yet they made it through four legs."

"Lada? Really?"

"Really. It's got a Chevy V8 in it. Frankenstein's monster on wheels."

The plane banked, and through the porthole, Tokyo appeared—a sprawl of neon and glass that seemed to glow from within, a city that never slept and never forgave. The sun was setting, painting the sky in streaks of orange and violet, and the city below was already flickering to life, a billion lights blinking like stars in a man-made galaxy.

"Night race," Dex said, pressing his palm against the cold glass. "They said bring light."

"Then we bring light."

The starting line was in Shibuya, in the middle of the crossing—the famous intersection where a thousand pedestrians flowed like water through concrete channels. But tonight, the crossing was empty. Barricaded. Closed. The holographic skull hovered above the scramble, its amber eyes sweeping the crowd of empty buildings, its mouth opening to reveal a screen that displayed the race parameters in brutal, flickering text:

LEG FIVE: TOKYO NIGHT RACE

COURSE: 47 KM — SHIBUYA TO ODAIBA

CHECKPOINTS: 5 — HOLOGRAPHIC

TWIST: THE CITY FIGHTS BACK

SPEED LIMIT: NONE. STOPLIGHTS: LIVE. PEDESTRIANS: REAL.

"Pedestrians real?" Dex read aloud, his voice climbing an octave. "The city's not closed?"

"It's Tokyo," Sable said, sliding into the passenger seat. "It's always open."

The Audi sat at the head of the pack, its headlights twin daggers cutting through the neon haze. Vega's Corvette idled to their right, the cracked windshield now replaced with something that looked like plexiglass—he'd made repairs during the flight, or someone had. The Bugatti twins were behind them, their car a silver bullet with gold accents, their faces hidden behind matching helmets. The Brazilian Evo growled like an animal on a leash. The Russian Lada sat at the back, its engine thumping, its driver—a woman with a shaved head and eyes like ice—staring straight ahead, unmoving.

And El Tapado. His Porsche was parked at an angle, half in shadow, its engine silent. The masked driver was leaning against the door, arms crossed, visor reflecting the neon like a black mirror.

"Who is he?" Dex muttered. "Nobody's seen his face. Nobody's heard his voice. Nobody's even sure he's human."

"Maybe he's not," Sable said. "Maybe that's the point."

The holographic skull blinked. The screen above the crossing flickered, and the race director's voice—gravel in a blender—filled the air, amplified by speakers hidden in the buildings.

"Welcome to Tokyo. The Night Race. Five checkpoints. Forty-seven kilometers. The slowest crew to cross the finish line is eliminated. But that's not the twist. The twist is this: the city is alive. Stoplights will turn against you. Crosswalks will fill with pedestrians. Police will chase you—not to stop you, but to slow you down. And at every checkpoint, you will face a challenge designed to break you, your car, or both."

The skull's eyes flared.

"Your first checkpoint is twelve kilometers away. The Rainbow Bridge. You have nine minutes. Go."

The world exploded.

The Bugatti took off like a missile, its engine a scream of pure violence. The Evo followed, the Lada close behind, Vega's Corvette surging with a roar that rattled the windows. Dex slammed the throttle, and the Audi launched, tires smoking on the asphalt, the crossing dissolving behind them into a smear of neon and shadow.

Shibuya became a blur. Buildings flickered past, their signs a kaleidoscope of kanji and color. Dex wove between a bus and a taxi, the Audi's mirrors folding automatically as he threaded a gap that shouldn't have existed. The streets were alive—not with traffic, but with people. Pedestrians on the sidewalks, in the crosswalks, some of them filming with phones, some of them just watching, their faces lit by the passing headlights.

"Stoplight ahead," Sable said, her eyes on her tablet. "It's green. But it's going to turn red in three seconds."

"Then I run it."

"Pedestrians."

Dex saw them—a cluster of people starting to step into the crosswalk, their arms full of shopping bags, their faces turned toward the approaching headlights. He slammed the brakes, the Audi skidding, the ABS shuddering, the car stopping inches from the first pedestrian—a young woman with pink hair and wide eyes who didn't even flinch.

"Sorry!" Dex shouted through the window, already flooring it again.

The woman gave him the finger. Fair enough.

The Rainbow Bridge loomed ahead, its towers reaching into the night like the ribs of a fallen god. The other crews were ahead—the Bugatti already on the bridge, its taillights twin rubies in the dark. Vega's Corvette was close behind, its engine howling. The Lada was somehow keeping pace, its Frankenstein heart pumping hatred and horsepower through a frame that looked like it should have rusted apart a decade ago.

"This is too easy," Sable said.

"Don't jinx it."

"I'm not. I'm just saying—the checkpoint is on the bridge. In the middle. Where the cables are."

"What about the cables?"

Sable's tablet lit up. A hologram flickered above the dash, showing the bridge from above. A glowing marker in the center, surrounded by a ring of red.

"The checkpoint is a loop. A full 360-degree turn around the support tower. In the middle of the bridge. At night."

Dex's hands tightened on the wheel. "How much room?"

"Twelve meters. Total. Between the tower and the railing."

Twelve meters. The Audi's turning radius was eleven point three. It would be a knife's edge, a hair's breadth, a prayer.

"We can do it," Sable said, reading his mind. "But we have to hit it at exactly the right speed. Too fast, we understeer into the railing. Too slow, we kiss the tower and die."

"Great options."

"Life's a gamble, Dex. Drive."

The tunnel opened onto the bridge, and the world expanded into a vast, glittering darkness. Tokyo sprawled on either side, a sea of lights, the bay glittering below. The Bugatti was already at the checkpoint—Dex watched as it swung around the tower, its rear end fishtailing, its tires screaming, the driver clipping the railing with a shower of sparks but completing the loop and roaring away.

Vega went next. The Corvette was heavier, less agile, and the ex-cop wrestled it around the tower like a man trying to tame a bull, the rear end sliding wide, the car spinning almost 360 degrees before Vega caught it, the nose kissing the tower with a sickening crunch before he straightened out and fled.

"Space cleared," Sable said. "Our turn."

Dex hit the brakes, the Audi dropping speed, the bridge rushing past on either side. The tower loomed ahead—a massive pillar of steel and concrete, a monument to engineering, and now a monument to his potential death.

"Eleven meters," Sable said. "Right now."

Dex cranked the wheel. The Audi pivoted, its rear end sliding wide, the tires howling, the scent of burning rubber filling the cabin. The tower rushed past on the left, so close Dex could see the rivets, the graffiti, the years of grime. The railing rushed past on the right, so close the mirrors scraped, sending a shower of sparks into the night.

And then they were through, the Audi straightening, the bridge opening ahead, the checkpoint complete.

"Thirty centimeters," Sable said, her voice a little shaky. "You had thirty centimeters on either side."

"Thirty centimeters is a mile in my book."

The comms crackled. The race director again.

"Checkpoint two. The Shuto Expressway. A maze of elevated roads. Twelve kilometers of twists, turns, and dead ends. But here's the twist: the expressway is closed tonight. Closed for maintenance. You'll be the only cars on it. And it's pitch black."

"Pitch black?" Dex repeated.

"Bring light," the director said, and the comms went dead.

The expressway entrance loomed ahead, a dark maw swallowing the road. No streetlights. No reflections. Just a black hole in the heart of the city, waiting to eat them alive.

Dex flicked on the Audi's high beams. They cut a narrow cone through the darkness, revealing ten meters of asphalt and nothing more.

"Twelve kilometers of this," Sable said.

"Then we drive blind."

"No, we drive smart. I'll guide you. The map's in my head. Left in two hundred meters. Right in four hundred. The expressway's a maze, but I've got the pattern."

"You memorized the Shuto Expressway?"

"I memorized every expressway on this continent, Dex. It's called being a professional."

"Or being insane."

"Same thing."

They dove into the darkness. The Audi's headlights carved a path through the void, the walls of the expressway rushing past, featureless and black. Sable called out directions—left, right, straight, merge—and Dex obeyed, his hands a blur on the wheel, his eyes fixed on the shrinking pool of light ahead.

Somewhere behind them, an engine roared. Another. The Russian Lada's headlights appeared in the rearview, twin suns in the dark, closing fast.

"They're catching up," Dex said.

"Let them. The expressway narrows ahead. Only one lane. They'll have to slow down."

The Lada didn't slow down. The Russian driver—the woman with the shaved head—plowed forward, her bumper inches from the Audi's rear, her headlights flooding the cabin with white-hot light.

"She's trying to push us," Sable said.

"Let her."

Dex slammed the brakes. The Audi stopped hard, the Lada rear-ending them with a crash of metal, the impact jolting through the frame. But the Lada's nose was now crumpled, its headlights shattered, and the Russian driver was momentarily blind.

"Now," Dex said, flooring it.

The Audi surged ahead, leaving the Lada in darkness. The expressway curved, twisted, and then opened into a wide stretch of elevated road with a view of the Tokyo skyline—a glittering panorama of lights that took Dex's breath away.

"Checkpoint three," Sable said, pointing.

Ahead, a holographic skull hovered over a massive jump—a ramp leading to a gap between two sections of elevated road, a gap that stretched at least thirty meters, with a two-story drop to the water below.

"Thirty meters," Dex said. "In the dark."

"Thirty meters. In the dark."

The Bugatti was already airborne, its silver body a bullet against the starry sky, landing with a perfect four-wheel bounce and roaring away. The Evo followed, its engine screaming, its landing less graceful—the rear end kicking out, the driver wrestling it back into control.

Then it was their turn. The gap loomed, a void of blackness and water.

"Full send," Sable said.

"Full send," Dex agreed.

He floored it. The Audi surged, the expressway blurring beneath them, the ramp approaching at a hundred and eighty kilometers per hour. The car launched, its nose tipping up, and for a moment, they were suspended in the air, the lights of Tokyo spinning around them, the water waiting below.

And then the landing—a crash, a bounce, a scream of suspension. The Audi hit the expressway on the other side, its wheels spinning, its frame groaning, but it held together. It always held together.

Behind them, the Lada hit the ramp too slow. The car clipped the edge of the gap, its front wheels catching, its body pitching forward. For a heart-stopping moment, the Lada hung there, balanced on its nose, the driver's eyes wide through the windshield.

And then it fell.

The Lada tumbled into the bay below, a splash of metal and light that vanished into the darkness. The comms crackled, and the race director's voice was flat, final.

"Three crews eliminated. Six remain."

Dex didn't look back. The expressway curved ahead, and the finish line—the rainbow glow of Odaiba—was waiting.

Somewhere behind them, El Tapado's Porsche was a silent shadow, slipping through the dark like a ghost.

The Night Race wasn't over. It had barely begun.

Chapter 16 — The Neon Grave

The rainbow glow of Odaiba grew in the windshield like a fever dream, the artificial island's Ferris wheel spinning slow circles of colored light against the black of Tokyo Bay. Dex's hands were welded to the wheel, his forearms screaming from the constant micro-corrections, the Audi's engine a steady, thrumming heartbeat that had carried them through twelve kilometers of pure darkness and one near-death experience on a ramp over open water.

"Six remain," Sable said, her voice flat. "That means three crews are already on the truck back to wherever they came from. With no cars."

"Don't think about them. Think about the next checkpoint."

Sable's tablet flickered. The hologram that rose from the dash was different this time—no single marker, but a trail of blinking dots snaking through a dense cluster of buildings. The image resolved, and Dex felt his stomach drop.

"Shibuya," he said.

"Shibuya Crossing. The most famous intersection on Earth. Five crosswalks, one giant scramble. And the entire district is the next leg."

"The race director wants us to drive through Shibuya Crossing?"

"Not through it," Sable said, zooming in. The hologram showed a series of gates, each one glowing with a number. "Around it. The checkpoint is a series of five gates. You have to hit them in order, and the shortest path between them is a lap around the crossing itself. Full circuits of the square. Like a roundabout from hell."

"How many laps?"

"Three."

"Three full loops around Shibuya Crossing. With other crews. At night."

"At night, on a closed course, with the whole world watching." Sable pointed to the corners of the hologram, where tiny camera icons flickered. "This is the showpiece leg. The one they'll broadcast. Every drone in Tokyo is going to be hovering over that intersection."

Dex laughed, a short, sharp sound that was half adrenaline, half dread. "So we're not just racing for survival. We're putting on a show."

"Speed is the only mercy, baby. But spectacle is the only currency."

The expressway exit ramp curved down, and the city swallowed them whole. The streets of Shibuya were lined with towering screens, all of them displaying the same thing—a live feed of the intersection ahead, with the five gates glowing in augmented reality, visible only through the car's displays and the drone cameras that followed every crew.

The crossing itself was a cathedral of concrete and neon. Eight lanes of asphalt converging on a single painted plaza, surrounded by department stores and video billboards, the screens casting everything in a shifting kaleidoscope of color—crimson, electric blue, acid green, gold. The gates materialized as glowing arches, each one pulsing with a countdown timer.

"Gate one is at the southwest corner," Sable said. "Gate two at the northeast. Gate three at the east entrance. Gate four at the west. Gate five back at the southwest—it's a loop."

"Three laps. Five gates. One intersection." Dex's fingers drummed the wheel. "What's the catch?"

"The gates are single-use. Once a car passes through, the gate goes down. If you miss your window, you have to wait for the next rotation."

"Rotation?"

The gates began to move. They weren't stationary arches—they were traveling, sliding along tracks embedded in the asphalt, circling the crossing in a slow, deliberate dance. The countdown timers were synchronized. Every thirty seconds, all five gates would align with their starting positions for exactly three seconds. Then they'd slide again, weaving through the intersection in a pattern that was almost certainly designed to cause chaos.

"Gate one aligns in ten seconds," Sable said. "Gate two aligns forty seconds after that. Gate three at one minute ten. Gate four at one minute forty. Gate five at two minutes ten. Then the cycle repeats."

"So we have to hit each gate in sequence, thirty seconds apart, while the gates are moving."

"Correct."

"And the other crews?"

As if on cue, the intersection filled with light. The Bugatti screamed in from the north, its silver body catching the neon like liquid mercury. The Porsche—El Tapado's ghost—slid in from the east, silent as a shadow. Vega's Corvette rumbled in from the south, its headlights cutting through the haze. And behind them, a fourth set of lights—the Evo, its driver a young woman with a buzz cut and a death wish, her car covered in sponsor stickers and battle scars.

"Five crews left," Sable said. "Us, the Bugatti, El Tapado, Vega, and the Evo."

"Where's the sixth?"

The answer came with the roar of an engine that sounded like a war crime. A Maserati MC20, painted matte black, its wheels spitting fire, slammed through a barricade on the east side of the crossing. The driver was a man Dex recognized from the pre-race briefing—a silver-haired Argentine with a scar across his jaw and a smile like a knife wound. The crew called him El Lobo. He'd been quiet all race, lurking in the middle of the pack, never too fast, never too slow. Now he was showing his teeth.

"El Lobo and his navigator," Sable said. "They've been sandbagging."

"Great. So we've got a ghost, a cop, a buzz-cut, a silver bullet, and a wolf. And we're racing through the most famous intersection on Earth, doing laps around a glowing gate system that wants to kill us."

"Don't forget the drones."

Dex looked up. The sky above Shibuya was thick with them—hundreds of quadcopters, their lights blinking in the dark like artificial stars, their cameras recording every second of this madness for the world to see.

"I haven't forgotten," he said. "Let's give them a show."

The countdown on Gate One hit zero. The arch glowed white, and the Bugatti surged forward, its driver threading the needle with surgical precision, the car passing through the gate with centimeters to spare before the arch began to slide again.

"Gate one is moving," Sable said. "Next alignment in twenty seconds. We need to be there when it hits."

Dex floored it. The Audi shot across the crossing, weaving through the painted lanes, the holographic gates flickering around them. The Bugatti was already on Gate Two, its driver showing off now, drifting around the corner of the intersection with the rear end kicked out, the tires smoking, the crowd of drones buzzing overhead like angry bees.

"Gate one is aligning," Sable said. "Now!"

Dex cranked the wheel. The Audi slid sideways, the rear end swinging around, and for a moment, they were perpendicular to the gate, the glowing arch dead ahead. He straightened, hit the gas, and passed through with a millisecond to spare—the arch sliding away behind them as the countdown reset.

"Gate one down. Gate two aligns in forty seconds. We're in second place."

"Behind the Bugatti?"

"Behind the Bugatti. He's already on Gate Three."

The Bugatti was a machine of pure arrogance, its driver taking every gate with a clean, clinical precision that was almost insulting. No flair, no drama. Just speed and accuracy, the car a silver dart through the neon chaos.

"Gate two aligning," Sable said. "But we've got company."

The Maserati was closing fast, El Lobo's face visible through the windshield, that scarred smile fixed on his lips. He pulled up beside the Audi, matching speed, the two cars side by side in the middle of the crossing.

"Don't let him push you wide," Sable said. "The gate is right ahead."

Dex held his line. The Maserati edged closer, its mirror inches from the Audi's. El Lobo was playing chicken, daring Dex to flinch.

"Gate two is aligning in five seconds," Sable said. "If we miss it, we wait thirty seconds and lose a lap."

Dex made a decision. He slammed the brakes. The Maserati shot ahead, El Lobo's eyes widening as he realized what had happened—he was now in the perfect position to take Gate Two, but the arch was on the wrong side of the intersection. He'd have to loop around.

Dex cranked the wheel, the Audi pivoting, and hit the gas. The car surged through the gap between the Maserati and the gate, passing through the arch with a hair's breadth to spare as the countdown hit zero and the gate began to slide away.

"That was reckless," Sable said.

"That was beautiful," Dex corrected. "Gate three?"

"Forty seconds. The Bugatti's already there. And El Tapado is on Gate Four."

The Porsche was a ghost, moving through the intersection with an otherworldly grace, its driver invisible behind the tinted glass. It took Gate Four with a perfect drift, the rear end sweeping wide, the car straightening out and accelerating away before the arch had even finished aligning.

"Who is that guy?" Dex wondered aloud.

"No one knows. That's the point. Gate three in fifteen seconds."

The Bugatti took Gate Three and vanished into the streets beyond Shibuya, its job done for this leg. The Evo was next, the buzz-cut driver taking the gate with a scream of tires and a shower of sparks. El Lobo was recovering, the Maserati looping around the crossing, its engine howling.

"Gate three aligning," Sable said. "Now."

Dex took it clean, the Audi passing through the arch as the countdown hit zero. The hologram flickered, marking the gate as complete.

"One lap down. Two to go."

The second lap was chaos. The gates were moving faster now, their rotation speed increasing with each cycle, the archways sliding through the intersection in an ever-shifting pattern that seemed designed to break minds. El Lobo had caught up, the Maserati and the Audi trading paint as they fought for position. Vega's Corvette was in the mix too, the ex-cop driving with a controlled fury that was almost frightening to watch.

"Gate one aligns in ten seconds," Sable said. "But El Lobo is going to be there first."

"Let him have it. We'll take the next rotation."

The Maserati took Gate One, and Dex let him go, pulling back, letting the arch slide away. The countdown reset. Thirty seconds. The intersection was a whirlwind of light and sound, the drones buzzing overhead, the billboards flashing with live footage of the race itself—Dex caught a glimpse of the Audi on a massive screen, the car a blur of red and black, and for a moment, he felt like he was watching someone else.

"Gate one aligning," Sable said. "Now."

The Audi surged forward, passing through the arch as it began to slide. The countdown hit zero just as the rear bumper cleared the glow.

"Gate two in forty seconds. We're on pace."

"On pace for what?"

"Second place. The Bugatti's already finished all three laps. He's gone."

"Of course he is." Dex gritted his teeth. "We're not racing for first this leg. We're racing for survival."

"El Tapado is on his final lap. Vega is on his second. El Lobo is on his second. The Evo is on her second. We're on our second. It's tight."

The third lap was a knife fight. Every crew was pushing, the intersection a maelstrom of engines and tires, the gates sliding faster, the countdowns shorter. The Evo clipped a gate as it moved, the buzz-cut driver's car spinning, the rear end slamming into a barrier with a crunch of metal. She was out—the car disabled, the crew eliminated.

"Four crews left," Sable said. "Us, El Tapado, Vega, and El Lobo."

"Where's the Bugatti?"

"Finished. He's waiting at the next checkpoint."

"Of course he is."

Gate five was the last one—the loop back to the southwest corner. Dex took it with everything he had, the Audi's engine screaming, the tires smoking, the arch glowing ahead. He passed through with two seconds to spare, and Sable's voice was triumphant.

"Three laps complete. We're done."

"Where do we finish?"

The hologram showed a marker at the far edge of Shibuya—a rooftop parking garage, the finish line glowing green. Dex floored it, the Audi tearing through the neon streets, the drones following like a swarm of hungry birds.

The parking garage was a concrete monolith, its ramps spiraling upward into the night. Dex took the entrance at speed, the Audi's tires screeching as he climbed, level after level, the city falling away below.

At the top, the finish line was a holographic banner, glowing green against the dark sky. The Bugatti was already there, parked, its driver leaning against the hood with a smug smile. El Tapado's Porsche was pulling in behind them, silent as ever. Vega's Corvette rumbled up a moment later.

The Maserati never arrived.

The race director's voice crackled over the comms. "Four crews eliminated. Four remain. The next leg begins at dawn. You'll have twelve hours to rest, repair, and prepare. The destination: Seoul. The challenge: the Gwangjang Market. A maze of stalls, alleys, and dead ends. And the twist this time?"

The director paused. The silence stretched, heavy with anticipation.

"The course is invisible. No holograms. No markers. You'll have to find your own way."

The comms went dead.

Dex looked at Sable. She was staring at her tablet, her face pale.

"What is it?" he asked.

She turned the screen toward him. It showed a map of Seoul, the market district highlighted in red. And in the center, pulsing like a heartbeat, was a single word.

"Garage."

"We're racing for the garage," Dex said.

"Not yet," Sable said. "But we're getting close."

The night was over. The dawn was coming. And somewhere in Seoul, a golden garage was waiting to claim its first victims.
